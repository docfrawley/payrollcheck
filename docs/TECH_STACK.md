# Tech stack

- `backend/`: FastAPI with SQLAlchemy Core, so each query is written once for both databases. With `DATABASE_URL` in its environment it uses the Supabase Postgres database through the session pooler, with every table in the `payrollcheck` schema and at most 4 connections, waiting up to two minutes for one; otherwise SQLite, as in the tests. `main.py` holds the app and the case and reviewer operations; `projects.py`, `runs.py`, `configuration.py` and `improvement.py` are `APIRouter`s, the first with the projects and the upload of a payroll (`intake.py`, the template's strict reader with no model), with the operations on dispatches and evaluations, on the payroll agent's configuration and settings, and on the improver's rounds; they share the `Database` and `SignedIn` dependencies in `dependencies.py` and the case views in `views.py`. The payroll agent's configuration (its instructions, model, reasoning effort and the check tools' tunable parameters, from the catalogue in `parameters.py` with their bounds, the rounding tolerance alone for now) and the improver's recipe are versioned records the backend owns, with diffs from `difflib` and each parameter's change before and after (`diffs.py`), and the backend applies the rule that keeps or rolls back a version (`verdict.py`): a table of six measures, the catch rate, false exceptions per 100 payrolls, shortfall accuracy and the notice score compared as differences against floors and a ceiling, and cost and time per case as fractions of the old value. Every API operation has an `operation_id`, a one-line docstring and no optional inputs. The tables and every query are in `database.py`; seeding, in `seeding.py`, loads `data/`'s committed output and recomputes nothing. Money is in integer cents and hours exact decimals. A case is one subcontractor's payroll for one project and one week, Received, In progress, Awaiting review, Accepted or Returned to sub, with its submissions, never changed, and its exceptions, each with its rule code from the catalogue in `rule_codes.py`, severity, worker, line, expected and actual, shortfall, citation and what raised it. `record_check_findings` keeps a check tool's findings as the attempt's candidates (`candidates.py`) and records the check as a step; `record_exception_report` checks a report against them (`reports.py`): an exception with a shortfall, or under a rule that carries money, must be a candidate of this attempt, every candidate is raised or dismissed with a reason, and a report with exceptions has a draft notice, each refusal a 422 that says what's wrong. `get_agent_api` (`GET /api/agent/openapi.json`) serves the OpenAPI document of the payroll agent's seven operations (`get_case`, `get_project`, `list_subcontractors`, `get_payroll_history`, `record_check_findings`, `record_screening_flag` and `record_exception_report`) for an AgentCore Gateway, built in `agent_api.py` with `get_openapi`, with no `servers` and no security schemes. An evaluation copies each case of a labelled set, development, held-out or tiny, as a Received case of its own with the submissions it had before review (`copies.py`), its payroll history from its own set (`history.py`), and `scoring.py` scores the copies against the labels strictly: a label is caught by an open exception with its rule code and worker, its shortfall correct to the cent, a screening label by a flag, and an exception matching no label, or a flag on a copy with none, is false. As an evaluation finishes, `grading.py` has the judge grade each copy's draft notice on four points, and `success_criteria.py` works out the business's four success criteria on a finished development-set evaluation, from its measures and the `decoys` table. `improver_view.py` is what the improver reads in a round: the scores, the development set's mistakes and weakest notices, and the reviewers' overrides and notice edits since the last round finished, each list capped with its total. The business date is a setting, or New Jersey's real date where it has none (`business_date.py`, with `zoneinfo` and the `tzdata` package, which the `server` group carries), and `GET /api/business-date` gives it to every signed-in reviewer for the header. `periods.py` resolves a period the Copilot names (last week, this month, the last N weeks or between two week endings) from it, with `data/payroll_world/periods.py`, which the generator's expected answers use too, always matched on a case's week ending. The Copilot's reads are a router of their own, `copilot_reads.py`, under `/api/copilot/`, for a signed-in reviewer in their scope: the queue's cases by filters (`case_search.py`), a case in full, its open exceptions, exposure (`exposure.py`), filings worked out from the engagement windows up to the business date (`filings.py`), classification changes against the week before (`classification_changes.py`), projects by name (`names.py`, shared with `subcontractors.py`), the success criteria (`criteria_report.py`) and the one write, `draft_notice`, which `notice_drafts.py` words from the case's open exceptions in each rule's own words (`rule_codes.py`'s `name` and `asks`). A filtered read is a POST whose every filter is required and null for any, and every amount comes in cents and as dollars (`money.cents_in_dollars`). Each answer that can hold a flagged case's text passes through `withheld.py`, the one place it is withheld: what the subcontractor wrote (`template.TEXT`), a worker id that isn't ours, the exceptions' and findings' expected and actual, the notices and the screening's reason. `reviewing.py` holds a case for a reviewer's work with a conditional update, shared by the case page's operations and the Copilot's draft. The backend does no rate arithmetic and reads nothing from `context/`. `get_screening` names the model the screening for hidden instructions runs on, `openai/gpt-6-luna` at `none` or the model in `MODEL_NAME`, outside the versions. The app keeps one piece of the agent's work going at a time (`agent_work.py`): a dispatch, an evaluation, or a round while the agent works it. A start updates a one-row `locks` table as its transaction's first write, so a second start waits and then sees the first's work; marks stopped the work the agent hasn't spoken of for five minutes (`heard_at`), a round with its running evaluation; refuses with a 409 naming what is going; creates the run or the round, or records the review; commits; and asks the agent to work it by its id, undoing what it did when the agent didn't take the work. The agent tells the app every minute while it works, and stops a run or a round whose work failed by its id. The backend asks the agent to work a run, to work a round and to carry a round on after a review through `agent_service.py`, one JSON payload per operation, on one of two routes chosen per call: its AgentCore app's `/invocations` at `PAYROLLCHECK_AGENT` or `http://127.0.0.1:8080`, with the agent's key as `Authorization: Bearer <key>` and a 10-second timeout, or, when `PAYROLLCHECK_AGENT_RUNTIME` names the agent's runtime, boto3's `invoke_agent_runtime` in the region the app runs in, with no key and a new session id for each call. It asks the Copilot to take a turn through `copilot_service.py`, with the reviewer's own access token in the payload: at `PAYROLLCHECK_COPILOT` or `http://127.0.0.1:8081`, with no key, or, when `PAYROLLCHECK_COPILOT_RUNTIME` names the Copilot's runtime, in the conversation's session. Both services send on `runtime_calls.py`, one attempt each try, 2 seconds to connect and 15 to read, retrying a `ConflictException` after 1, 2 and then 4 seconds while the wait and another whole try fit within 25 seconds, and keeping botocore's loggers at INFO or above, since their DEBUG lines hold each payload; `asking.py` commits what the app did for the work before it asks and undoes it when the work wasn't taken. The Copilot's conversations, their turns and each turn's cards are the backend's records (`conversations.py`, the `copilot.py` router): one turn at a time in a conversation, by a conditional update of its count of turns; a turn still working 20 minutes after its start reads as stopped and takes no more records; and a conversation found only for its own reviewer. `subcontractors.py` finds subcontractors by legal name. The agent didn't take the work when it was never reached (a 503, "The agent's service isn't running"), refused (a 409 with its reason) or gave an error answer, any status of 400 or more or an answer that is neither `{"accepted": true}` nor a refusal (a 502, "The agent couldn't take the work: ..."); a timeout or a cut-off answer once the request was sent may have started it (a 503 telling the reviewer to reload).
  - Who may call what: every route but `health` has one gate in `dependencies.py`. A request with the agent's key is the agent's: a reviewer's route refuses it with a 403, whatever else it carries, and `SignIn` gives a route the signed-in reviewer's own token to pass on to the Copilot. `AgentKey` needs the agent's API key, which the agent's side sends in an `X-API-Key` header and `api_key.py` compares with `PAYROLLCHECK_API_KEY` with `secrets.compare_digest`, read through FastAPI's `APIKeyHeader`. The reads both use (`list_cases`, `get_case`, `get_payroll_history`, `get_project`, `list_subcontractors` and `get_run`) take `ScopedOrAgent`, which lets through the key or a signed-in reviewer and gives the route the caller's scope, and `get_agent_api` takes `ReviewerOrAgent`, the same gate with no scope; every other route needs a signed-in reviewer. The key never stands in for a reviewer, so it never signs off, sends back, overrides an exception or saves a notice. With no key set, every route that needs it refuses.
  - Roles and scope: a person is a `reviewer` or the `compliance_manager` (`Role`), and `compliance_manager(action)` in `dependencies.py` refuses anyone else the Improvement and Settings reads and actions with a 403 that names the action. `scope.py` is the one place scope is defined: `scope_of` gives a reviewer the projects `project_reviewers` assigns them, and the compliance manager every one of ours, never a held-out project; the agent's key has `EVERYTHING`. Routes take `Scoped` or `ScopedOrAgent`, `lookups.py`'s `find_case` and `project_row` take the scope and answer an id outside it with a 404, the same as an unknown id, and every list filters with `cases_in`, `projects_in` or `subcontractors_in`. An evaluation's copy is in every scope. A dispatch works the whole queue; `find_run` shows each person their own cases, those cases' cost, the summary of them and `other_projects`, the count of the rest, with the run's own time from start to finish.
  - The review of a case: `overrides.py` dismisses, restores or adds an exception, each logged in `overrides` with who, when and why and never edited or deleted, `overrides_between` reading the log by date. `notices.py` keeps the payroll agent's or the Copilot's draft (`drafted_for` naming the reviewer a Copilot draft is for, which the judge's and the improver's readers leave out) and the reviewer's edit of a notice, both, and `outbox.py` records each notice sent, one for each send, with the draft it came from, and hands it to `deliver(outbox_row)`; a send carries the notice as the reviewer saw it, refused with a 409 when it has changed since (`outbox.send_notice`), and a notice on a case Returned to sub can be edited and sent again (`resend_notice`), the one place a real email provider would plug in. An override or a notice save first takes the case's row lock with a conditional update (`hold_for_review`), and Sign off and Send back move the case first, then read what they check or send, so none of them interleave. Intake's `reopen` makes a correction the case's next submission, back to Received as a fresh first attempt, with the earlier open exceptions superseded, in one conditional move.
  - Sign-in: Supabase Auth. `auth.py` checks each access token with PyJWT's `PyJWKClient` against the project's public keys at `VITE_SUPABASE_URL`, and the backend finds the reviewer by the token's email.
  - The judge: Jev (`typesafe/jev-1.13`) through OpenRouter's Decisions endpoint with the `openrouter` package, in `judge.py`, at `JUDGE_URL` or `https://openrouter.ai`, grades a draft send-back notice on four `noul` points (it covers every exception, its amounts match the records, it is in plain language, it gives no legal interpretation), its score their average, with a 10-second timeout and no retries. Only `grading.py` calls it, on a thread for each notice as an evaluation finishes, and never on a free model. `open_exceptions.py` is the one reader of the open exceptions on each copy's latest submission, which the scoring matches to the labels and the judge and the improver read beside a notice.
  - `free_models.py` is the one place that reads `MODEL_NAME`: with it set, seeding makes that model version 1 and the only model allowed, the improver uses it too, the judge isn't called, and each of a round's evaluations works the tiny set in place of the set it counts as (`runs.scored_as`).
- `agent/`, in parts kept apart. `connection.py` holds the one client to the backend, at `PAYROLLCHECK_API` or `http://127.0.0.1:8000`, and sends the agent's API key from `PAYROLLCHECK_API_KEY` with every request, read as each request is sent. `api.py` is the payroll agent's and the entry point's API on it: cases, projects, runs, the configuration's versions and the records of cost and time; `improver_api.py` is the improver's: the rounds, the results, proposals, versions, recipes and verdicts. `routes.py`, `gateway.py`, `direct.py` and `mcp_tools.py` are the payroll agent's two routes to its tools. `checks/` holds the eight check tools, pure functions of `CheckInputs` computing with `context/wage_rules.py`, and `check_inputs.py` reads `CheckInputs` on the route. `tools.py` holds the payroll agent's tools, the context they read and the payroll graph's state, and the tools read the case, its project, its subcontractor and the route from the agent's context through `ToolRuntime`. `harness.py` builds and runs the payroll agent. `dispatcher.py` works a dispatch or an evaluation. `improver.py` is the improver. `entrypoint.py` is the agent's AgentCore app (`BedrockAgentCoreApp` from `bedrock-agentcore`), one `@app.entrypoint` taking each operation's JSON payload, read with a Pydantic discriminated union on `operation` with no optional or extra inputs (a 400 says what's wrong): it answers `{"accepted": true}` at once and runs the work on a thread of its own, registered with `app.add_async_task` and completed however it ends, so the app's `/ping` says `HealthyBusy` while work goes on; `{"refused": "..."}` when it won't; and `{"healthy": true}` for the health check. Nothing happens as it's imported: the first request gets it ready, once in its process, and a failure answers 503 for the next request to try again. Off Runtime, every request but the health check needs the agent's key as `Authorization: Bearer <key>`. `work.py` is the work the entry point hands on: a run through the dispatcher and a round through the improver, each telling the app every minute. `identity.py` fetches the agent's credentials on Runtime: the deployment names each provider in `AGENTCORE_CREDENTIAL_<NAME>_NAME`, and `requires_api_key` fetches its value into `<NAME>`. `heartbeat.py` tells the app every minute that a piece of work is still going. `runtime/main.py`, the platform team's, runs the app on AgentCore Runtime. `screening.py` is the screening for instructions hidden in any text cell of a payroll, after `labs/week3day2_lab1.ipynb`. `console.py` sets up the report lines and says in one line what went wrong.
  - The payroll agent's tools and its check node's reads take one route, read when a run's payroll agents are built: `gateway.py`, the AgentCore Gateway's MCP tools at `PAYROLLCHECK_GATEWAY_URL`, named `payrollcheck-app___<operation>` and `payrollcheck-context___<tool>` and signed with SigV4 for `bedrock-agentcore` with the AWS CLI's credentials, or else `direct.py`, the app through `api.py` with the key and the context layer over MCP at `PAYROLLCHECK_CONTEXT` or `http://127.0.0.1:8006/mcp`, unsigned. Both are what `routes.py` says a route is, and `mcp_tools.py` calls MCP tools with `MultiServerMCPClient` from `langchain-mcp-adapters`, with `handle_tool_errors=False`, each call on an event loop of its own that leaves the thread's loop alone, in a session of its own. The tools fill in the case, its project, its subcontractor and who raises the exceptions from the agent's context, and a call the Gateway refuses comes back to the model as a refusal. The bookkeeping and the screening's read go straight to the app with the key on either route.
  - The payroll agent is a hand-written LangGraph `StateGraph`, built by `payroll_graph(llm, screener, saver, instructions)`: first the screening for hidden instructions (`screen`), on the model `get_screening` names, which reads every text cell of the latest submission with Structured Outputs in a strict tool call and has a `RetryPolicy` for an answer it can't read, then `flag` for a case it flags, which goes to Awaiting review with the screening's reason and never reaches the model, or else `check`, which runs the eight check tools in order and records each one's findings, then a node that calls the model and a `ToolNode`, until the model records its exception report. There is no pause, no payment and no memory. Its nodes record each reply, tool call (named as the route names it), tool result, refusal and lookup with its sources on the case, and each model call's tokens and cost, return refusals to the model and limit the model calls. It saves its state after every step with LangGraph's `PostgresSaver` in the same Supabase database, with `durability="sync"`, so the agent always needs `DATABASE_URL`, even when the backend runs on SQLite. Each case run gets a saver of its own on the process's shared `ConnectionPool` of at most 4 connections, since a saver lets one case at a time read or write. The agent is synchronous throughout, because async Postgres connections don't work with Windows' default event loop.
  - The dispatcher is a LangGraph `StateGraph` with no model, in the style of `labs/week2day4.ipynb`: `list_cases` lists the cases, `fan_out` sends each to the `work_case` node with a `Send`, `max_concurrency` caps the payroll agents at once at N, and `finish_run` runs once after them all. The `work_case` node has a `RetryPolicy` for rate limits and timeouts. It saves no progress of its own.
  - The harness's entry points are `work_case(case_id, run_id, agent)`, which works one case as a new attempt, `carry_on(case_id, run_id, agent)`, which carries a case on from its saved progress, and `work_in_run(case_id, run_id, new_agent)`, the one function the dispatcher calls, which does either with a payroll agent of its own: a `PayrollAgent`, its graph with a saver of its own, its route, who raises its exceptions and its version's parameters, from which each case's context is built. Each times its spells of work and records them with their run.
  - Every payroll graph run is traced in Langfuse, in a session named after the case and its thread, and tagged with its run's id in `langfuse_tags`.
  - The payroll agents of a run use its version's model and reasoning effort, `openai/gpt-6-luna` with no reasoning (`none`) in version 1, so a higher reasoning effort is an improvement a round can find. Each model call's cost comes from OpenRouter's `usage.cost` on the reply. `uv run -m agent` reads `PAYROLLCHECK_API` from the shell's environment, never `.env`; the agent's AgentCore app gets `.env` from `npm run dev`.
  - The improver is a LangChain `create_agent` on the round's model, `openai/gpt-6.1-sol` at medium by default, with the lab's four tools; `propose_change` takes one `change`: the instructions, model, reasoning effort, parameters, reason and the case ids behind it, which the backend checks against what the round's view holds. `HumanInTheLoopMiddleware` pauses it for a reviewer's approval, with its progress saved by a `PostgresSaver` of its own on the round's thread; `ModelCallLimitMiddleware` caps its model calls in a round at 20; parallel tool calls are off. Each stretch of its work is traced in Langfuse in the round's session.
- `agent/copilot/`: the PayrollCheck Copilot, an agent for the reviewers, built like the payroll agent and kept apart from it. `app_api.py` is its API on the app, every request carrying the reviewer's sign-in through `connection.call_as` and never the agent's key. `graph.py` is a hand-written LangGraph `StateGraph`: `begin`, with a memory `recall`, the model (`agent`), a `ToolNode` whose `wrap_tool_call` records each step as a card with the app (`cards.py`), and with a memory `remember`. Its tools (`tools.py`) are thin calls to the backend's Copilot reads, a filtered read taking its filters as one object (`filters.py`, a period by name as a discriminated union), with the five payroll lookups, `draft_notice`, `start_dispatch`, `start_evaluation`, `wait_for_run`, `get_latest_dispatch` and `check_success_criteria`; none signs off, sends back or overrides a case. Each turn reads the business date from the app, which the instructions give the model. The reviewer's sign-in reaches the nodes and the tools in LangGraph's context (`CopilotContext`, read through `ToolRuntime`) and is never saved. It saves its state after every step with `PostgresSaver` on the conversation's thread, with `durability="sync"`, runs one tool at a time and makes at most 25 model calls a turn, on the model `get_copilot_model` names, `openai/gpt-6-luna` at `low` or the model in `MODEL_NAME`. `turns.py` checks the turn with the app first, closes any tool call a stopped turn left open, runs the graph and tells the app how the turn ended. `waiting.py` waits in code for a dispatch or an evaluation it started, every 5 seconds for up to 15 minutes; `needs_a_reviewer.py` works out what needs a reviewer from the Copilot's search of every case in scope: those awaiting review with open exceptions or none, flagged, failed and Received. `memory.py` is its memory of each reviewer, `AgentCoreMemoryStore` at `PAYROLLCHECK_COPILOT_MEMORY_ID`, searched in `("reviewers", <reviewer>)` and saved with the reviewer as the actor and the conversation as the session. `entrypoint.py` is its AgentCore app, one `@app.entrypoint` taking `health` and `take_turn` (with `conversation_id`, `turn_id` and `sign_in`), read with a Pydantic discriminated union: it gets ready on its first request (on Runtime fetching `OPENROUTER_API_KEY` and `DATABASE_URL` from AgentCore Identity), checks the sign-in and the turn with the app before it answers, keeps one turn at a time for each conversation in its process, and works the turn on a thread of its own, registered as an async task. It asks for no key. The two AgentCore apps share `agentcore_app.py`; the two agents share `checkpoints.py`, `tool_route.py`, `context_tools.py`, `agentcore_memory.py`, `app_terms.py` (the app's words for its records) and `refusals.py` (how a model is told a refusal).
- `context/`: the context layer, an MCP server of its own (FastMCP from the `mcp` package, stateless with JSON responses) with the five payroll files. `sources.py` says what every answer names: each file with its system, owner and review date. `payroll_files.py` reads the payroll files once (the rate determinations, the contractor registry and the apprentice registrations, which `npm run generate` writes, and the filing rules and the payroll glossary, written by hand, each marked "Synthetic data — not real rates") and answers five: `get_wage_rate`, `check_registration`, `check_apprentice`, `get_filing_rules` and `lookup_payroll_term`, an unknown craft, classification, county or registration refused with its reason. `registrations.py` says what a contractor's or an apprentice's registration says on a date, for the lookups and the generator alike. `wage_rules.py` is the one rate logic, standard library only, money in integer cents: the determination in force on a project's award date and its latest row on or before a work date, an apprentice's rate as a percentage of the journeyman row, a week's hours split into straight and premium time by the craft's overtime rules (as data: over 8 hours a day, Saturdays, Sundays and holidays) with the 40-hour weekly floor, and a line's base, fringe and overtime shortfalls, rounded half up to the cent once. The generator, the context layer and the check tools compute every rate and shortfall with it. `server.py` serves the five lookups as tools, `uv run -m context` at `http://127.0.0.1:8006/mcp` and `context.server.handler` on Lambda through Mangum, with a fresh server for each request. Both agents have the five lookups.
- `dashboard/`: React, Vite and TypeScript, with icons from `lucide-react`. `auth.ts` is the one module that uses `@supabase/supabase-js`, with `VITE_SUPABASE_URL` and `VITE_SUPABASE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY`, which Vite reads from the repo root's `.env` in development and production mode, never in the tests. The app fills the window, the header across the top and the page scrolling on its own beside the Copilot's sidebar, with the page's layouts answering to the width the page has through CSS container queries. The sidebar (`components/Copilot.tsx` and its parts) reads a conversation through `useLoad` about once a second while a turn works, and sends a message through `useAction` with a sign-in `auth.freshAccessToken()` renews first when it has less than 20 minutes left; answers are formatted with `react-markdown` and `remark-gfm`, case ids linked by a remark plugin on `mdast-util-find-and-replace`. What the browser remembers of the sidebar for each reviewer is in `localStorage`.
- `e2e/`: Playwright tests, with stand-ins for the agent's AgentCore app (`/invocations` and `/ping`), the Copilot's, which plays scripted turns through the backend with the reviewer's sign-in, and Supabase Auth.
- `data/`: the business data the backend starts from. The agent never sees `data/evals/`, which holds the answer key. `data/generate.py` (`npm run generate`) builds PayrollCheck's synthetic payroll world from fixed seeds and writes it, the same on every run: `data/payroll_world/` draws a world (`world.py`, from the specs in `worlds.py`), pays every expected filing correctly (`clean.py`), applies a hand-written error manifest (`manifest.py`, `corruptions.py`, `decoys.py`) and makes each filing its case (`lifecycle.py`, `cases.py`), and writes the records in the CSV template (`template.py`) and JSON (`records.py`), the labels and the Copilot's expected answers (`expected_answers.py`, with every period from `periods.py`, which the backend reuses in stage 6). It imports nothing from the app, and a test fails when the committed files differ from what it writes. `data/development/arrivals/` holds the two missing filings' payrolls, for a reviewer to upload.

Python 3.12 with uv, all in the root `pyproject.toml`, where the `runtime` dependency group holds exactly what the agent imports on AgentCore Runtime (`bedrock-agentcore`, `boto3`, `botocore`, `httpx`, `langchain`, `langchain-core`, `langchain-openai`, `langchain-mcp-adapters`, `langfuse`, `langgraph`, `langgraph-checkpoint-postgres`, `langgraph-checkpoint-aws`, `mcp`, `psycopg[binary]`, `psycopg-pool`, `pydantic` and `starlette`), which `npm run check:runtime` checks, and the `server` group has `boto3` for the Runtime route. Node 26. `.env` holds the OpenRouter key (`OPENROUTER_API_KEY`), the Supabase session pooler connection string (`DATABASE_URL`), the Supabase project's address and publishable key (`VITE_SUPABASE_URL`, `VITE_SUPABASE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY`), the reviewers' password for browser checks (`REVIEWER_PASSWORD`), the agent's API key (`PAYROLLCHECK_API_KEY`), the Langfuse keys (`LANGFUSE_SECRET_KEY`, `LANGFUSE_PUBLIC_KEY` and `LANGFUSE_BASE_URL`), and optionally `JUDGE_URL`, `MODEL_NAME`, `PAYROLLCHECK_GATEWAY_URL`, `PAYROLLCHECK_CONTEXT` and `PAYROLLCHECK_COPILOT_MEMORY_ID`.

## Deployment

- `npm run build` builds the dashboard into `dashboard/dist`, with `VITE_SUPABASE_URL` and `VITE_SUPABASE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY` from the environment or, where it doesn't set them, from `.env`, so the build works on a server with no `.env`. When `dashboard/dist` exists, the backend serves it: its files at their own paths and `index.html` for any other path outside `/api/` (`backend/dashboard.py`).
- The package holds `backend/` and `data/`, without `tests/` or `__pycache__`, `dashboard/dist/`, and the `server` dependency group of `pyproject.toml` installed for Linux and Python 3.12 (`uv export --only-group server`, then `uv pip install --target ... --python-platform x86_64-manylinux2014 --python-version 3.12 --only-binary :all:`). `npm run check:server` checks that the backend imports with only that group installed. At run time the backend imports nothing from outside the package, reads files only from it, and writes none. The check tools run in the agent, so the backend's package needs nothing from `context/`. `data/payroll_world/periods.py`, which its Copilot tools use from stage 6, is in the package with `data/`.
- The context layer's package holds `context/`, without `tests/` or `__pycache__`, and the `context` dependency group (`mcp`, `mangum` and `pyyaml`) installed for Linux and Python 3.12 in the same way. Its handler is `context.server.handler`, behind a Function URL with `AWS_IAM` auth, so only callers allowed to invoke it can reach it, the Gateway among them, with no environment variables. It reads files only from its own package, reads them once when the function starts, builds a fresh MCP server for each request, and writes nothing. `npm run check:context` checks that it imports with only the `context` group installed.
- On AWS Lambda the handler is `backend.main.handler` (Mangum, with the startup hook off, so nothing seeds the database). On a web server the app is `backend.main:app`, as in `uv run -m uvicorn backend.main:app`.
- The agent runs on AgentCore Runtime, or on the laptop with `npm run dev`. The AgentCore project in `agentcore/` deploys two runtimes, `agent` and `copilot`, with one `agentcore deploy`, as `payrollcheck_default_agent` and `payrollcheck_default_copilot`: its package is `runtime/` with `agent/` beside it, without the tests, and `context/__init__.py` and `context/wage_rules.py`, the rate logic the check tools import, and the `runtime` group's packages at `uv.lock`'s versions, installed for Linux on ARM and Python 3.12, on Runtime's platform version V2. Runtime starts `runtime/main.py` in each of the agent's sessions, which runs the agent's AgentCore app, and the runtime's role may call the Gateway (`runtime/policy.json`); it starts `runtime/copilot.py` in each of the Copilot's sessions, one for each conversation, and the Copilot's role may call the Gateway and use the Copilot's Memory, `payrollcheck_copilot_memory` (`runtime/copilot_policy.json`). The Copilot fetches `OPENROUTER_API_KEY` and `DATABASE_URL` from AgentCore Identity on its first request, and gets `PAYROLLCHECK_API`, `PAYROLLCHECK_GATEWAY_URL` and `PAYROLLCHECK_COPILOT_MEMORY_ID` from `agentcore/.env.local`; on Runtime its context layer tools always go through the Gateway. The Copilot's Memory is created by `labs/week3day5_lab1.ipynb`, with a User Preference strategy on `/reviewers/{actorId}/preferences/` and a Semantic strategy on `/reviewers/{actorId}/facts/`. On Runtime, `OPENROUTER_API_KEY`, `PAYROLLCHECK_API_KEY` and `DATABASE_URL` (Supabase's transaction pooler, port 6543) are credentials in AgentCore Identity, which the agent fetches on its first request; `PAYROLLCHECK_API` and `PAYROLLCHECK_GATEWAY_URL` come from `agentcore/.env.local` when `agentcore deploy` runs. There the agent's tools always go through the Gateway, since only the Gateway can reach the context layer, and the runtime's own role signs its requests to the Gateway. The agent's settings beside `.env`'s on the laptop: `PAYROLLCHECK_GATEWAY_URL`, the AgentCore Gateway its payroll agents' tools go through when it's set; and `PAYROLLCHECK_CONTEXT`, the context layer on the direct route, `http://127.0.0.1:8006/mcp` when it isn't set. Whenever `PAYROLLCHECK_GATEWAY_URL` is set, the agent uses the AWS CLI's credentials and region, which sign the Gateway's requests.
- The function's environment is `DATABASE_URL` on Supabase's transaction pooler (port 6543), `VITE_SUPABASE_URL`, `PAYROLLCHECK_API_KEY`, `PAYROLLCHECK_AGENT_RUNTIME`, the ARN of the agent's runtime, and `PAYROLLCHECK_COPILOT_RUNTIME`, the ARN of the Copilot's, which the function's role may invoke (`bedrock-agentcore:InvokeAgentRuntime`). With `AWS_LAMBDA_FUNCTION_NAME` set, as Lambda sets it, the backend opens a connection for each request and keeps none (SQLAlchemy's `NullPool`), with prepared statements off (psycopg's `prepare_threshold=None`), which the transaction pooler needs.
