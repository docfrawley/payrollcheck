- **Phase 1, the tool parameters in the configuration:** `backend/parameters.py` is the catalogue of the check tools' tunable parameters, each with its label, unit, bounds and version 1's value: the rounding tolerance alone, a whole number of cents from 0 to 100, 0 in version 1 (decisions 3 and 4). `refuse_out_of_bounds` refuses with a 422 a set of parameters with one missing, one unknown, or a value that isn't a whole number inside its bounds; phase 4 uses it when the improver proposes and when a version is made. `versions.motivating_cases` holds the cases an approved proposal cited, none for version 1. On the Configuration API each version gives its parameters with their labels, the parameters it changed before and after (`diffs.parameter_changes`), and its motivating cases; `get_version`, which the payroll agent reads, still gives its parameters as data and no scores or motivating cases. The dashboard's shapes of a diff and of the parameters are in `dashboard/src/changes.ts`.

- **Phase 2, the measures and the keep-or-roll-back rule:** the settings hold an objective for four measures (catch rate at least 0.92, false exceptions at most 2.5 per 100 payrolls, shortfall accuracy at least 1.00, notice score at least 0.80) and a noise and tolerance for six (those four, and cost and seconds per case), as the brief's table, with a spending limit of $5.00 a round, seeded from `data/improvement.json`. `backend/verdict.py` is a table of the six measures, each better higher or lower, compared as a difference or a fraction of the old value, with a floor, a ceiling or no objective (decision 10): a version with no value for a measure the current version has, such as no notice score, is rolled back; a measure only the new version has must meet its objective but isn't compared; one neither has is left out. Gains, noise and tolerance are compared to 9 decimal places, so a score exactly at a noise, a tolerance or an objective, such as the average of two runs, ties rather than missing by a rounding error. `Measures` has `notice_score` and `notices_graded`, null and 0 until phase 3 grades notices. The Settings page shows and saves the objective, noise and tolerance of each measure in its unit: false exceptions per 100 payrolls, the notice score from 0 to 1. The dashboard's settings shapes are in `dashboard/src/settings.ts`. `test_the_app_hears_from_a_round_while_it_works` keeps three labelled cases a set, since the old $0.50 had been cutting its round short.

- **Phase 3, the judge's notice rubric:** `backend/judge.py` asks Jev four `noul` questions about a draft notice and the open exceptions it goes with, each the chance it keeps to a point of the rubric: it covers every exception, its amounts match the records, it is in plain language a payroll clerk can act on, and it gives no legal interpretation. The account lets 10 Lambda functions run at once, and with 10 payroll agents the app's function was throttled 3 times that minute; Lambda's 429 has no `detail`, so the agent's connection failed with a `KeyError` rather than a refusal. `agent/connection.py` now raises `Throttled` on a 429, which the harness counts with the model's rate limits and timeouts as passing with time (`TRANSIENT`), so the dispatcher's `RetryPolicy` waits and carries the case on; a case the try finds already worked, as it is when the throttled request came after its report, is reported as it stands, not started again; and any other refusal without a `detail` gives the body as it came. This covers the agent's own calls to the app, the steps, model calls, starts, failures and reads of the case, which PC-0704's was. A throttle on the Gateway's route, the tools, the checks' inputs and findings and the flag, comes back from the Gateway as its refusal, and is still treated as one: the model is told, or the case fails and goes back in the queue for its second attempt. Raising the account's Lambda concurrency is a request in Service Quotas, the user's to make. Stage 7's dispatch at 5 payroll agents at once wasn't throttled, with at most 7 of the app's functions running at once, so an evaluation on AWS at 5 or fewer likely stays under the limit, though the context layer's function, the heartbeats and the dashboard share it.
  - The judge couldn't grade notices on AWS: step 2 gave the app's function no `OPENROUTER_API_KEY`, which the judge, in the backend, needs, and the smoke check's dispatch grades nothing. Step 2 now gives it.

## Built

- `backend/`: the database is seeded from `data/` by `seeding.py`, and created on startup when it has no tables. Only seeding reads `data/evals/`, and nothing reads `context/`. A test checks that every operation follows the API rules. `main.py` holds the app and the case and reviewer operations; `projects.py` is an `APIRouter` with the projects and the upload of a payroll; `runs.py` one with every operation under `/api/runs`, `/api/dispatches` and `/api/evaluations`; `configuration.py` one with the payroll agent's versions and the settings; `improvement.py` one with the rounds of the improver, the agent's operations under `/api/rounds` and the dashboard's under `/api/improvement`; and `subcontractors.py` and `copilot.py` the Copilot's reads and records. They share the `Database` and `SignedIn` dependencies in `dependencies.py`, `to_case()` and `to_summary()` in `views.py`, finding a run or a version and the not-found messages in `lookups.py`, reading runs in `scoreboard.py` and `now()` in `database.py`, and no router uses another's helpers. Case and run statuses, run kinds and evaluation sets are `StrEnum`s in `models.py`.
  - On AWS Lambda: `handler` at the end of `backend/main.py` is the app through Mangum, with the startup hook off, so nothing seeds the database there. With `AWS_LAMBDA_FUNCTION_NAME` set, `engine_for` opens Postgres with a connection for each request and none kept (`NullPool`) and prepared statements off (`prepare_threshold=None`), for Supabase's transaction pooler on port 6543. `backend/dashboard.py` serves `dashboard/dist` through a Starlette `StaticFiles` that gives `index.html` for any page it has no file for, on a `Mount` after every route that takes no path under `/api/`, and none while the dashboard isn't built: the API, its wrong methods, trailing slashes and unknown paths, and `/docs`, answer as they always have. `npm run check:server` imports the backend in a temporary environment with only the `server` dependency group, the packages the deployment installs.
  - Database: SQLAlchemy Core, with the tables and every query written once in `backend/database.py` for both databases. With `DATABASE_URL` set, it is the Supabase Postgres database, through psycopg and the session pooler, with every table in the `payrollcheck` schema (`search_path` is set on each connection, in place of any `options` in the URL and keeping its other settings, such as `sslmode`) and a pool of at most 4 connections, waiting up to two minutes for one. The key never stands in for a reviewer: the upload, Sign off and Send back refuse it. A test checks that the lists of operations cover the app exactly once and that each operation's OpenAPI security matches its gate.

  - Workflow operations: `start_case`, `record_step`, `record_check_findings`, `record_screening_flag`, `record_exception_report`, `fail_case`, `record_model_call` and `record_work_time` for the agent's key, and `sign_off_case`, `send_back_case` and `upload_payroll` for a signed-in reviewer. `record_step` takes each step's `sources`, the context files a lookup came from, and none for any other step; the kinds the agent records are `screening`, `tool_call`, `tool_result`, `message`, `lookup` and `refusal`, and the app records a `check` step for each check's findings and an `error` step for a failed attempt itself.

Each has the case's thread and the run it happened in, or none.
  - Moving a case: `start_case` moves a Received case to In progress as a new attempt on a new thread, once, with a conditional update. The report or the screening flag moves it to Awaiting review; `fail_case` records the reason as an `error` step and puts it back to Received, or after its second attempt to Awaiting review with `agent_failed`. Sign off and Send back each move a case Awaiting review to Accepted or Returned to sub with a conditional update, so of two at the same moment only one goes through, and record the reviewer and the business date as `reviewed_by` and `reviewed_on`; both refuse a copy with a 409, and Sign off refuses a case with an open exception on its latest submission, saying how many. The `Database` dependency opens one connection for a request and commits at its end, and a route can commit sooner with `db.commit()`, as a start does before it asks the agent.
  - Starting: `start_dispatch` (`POST /api/dispatches`, body `{agents}`) and `start_evaluation` create the run, with an evaluation's copies, through `backend/agent_work.py`: the lock (an update of the one row in `locks`, the transaction's first write), quiet work marked stopped (a run, or a round with its running evaluation, whose `heard_at` is more than five minutes old, "The agent stopped answering"; a round's evaluation is never stopped on its own), a 409 naming the work going ("A dispatch is running", "An evaluation is running", "Round 3 is running"), the run, `heard_at`, a commit, then `work_run` for the agent, and 202. When the agent didn't take the work the run is stopped with the reason and the undo committed before the refusal; a timeout changes nothing, so the run stays running until it goes quiet. The agent's refusal is a 409 with its reason, an agent that can't be reached a 503 "The agent's service isn't running", an error answer a 502 "The agent couldn't take the work: ...", and a timeout or a cut-off answer a 503 telling the reviewer to reload. `agent_service.py` sends each operation's payload to `/invocations` at `PAYROLLCHECK_AGENT` or `http://127.0.0.1:8080` with the key as `Authorization: Bearer <key>`, or, when `PAYROLLCHECK_AGENT_RUNTIME` names the agent's runtime, with boto3's `invoke_agent_runtime`, a new session each call, one attempt, 2 seconds to connect and 15 to read, through `runtime_calls.py`; it reads the answer's `statusCode` before its body, and an answer that is neither an acceptance nor a refusal is an error answer.
  - Runs: a run is a dispatch or an evaluation, with a number, its N payroll agents at once, the version of the payroll agent it used with that version's model and reasoning effort, which the backend records when the run is created, a status (running, finished or stopped), its start and finish, and its cases in order in `run_cases`. A reviewer's start creates them; the agent reads one with `get_run`, records its cases and finishes it with `record_run_cases` and `finish_run`, tells the app it's still working with `record_run_heartbeat`, and stops one whose work failed with `fail_run`, under `/api/runs`. A stopped run keeps why in `stopped_because`: the agent's refusal or why it couldn't be reached, the error from `fail_run`, "The agent stopped answering", its round's reason, or the spending limit that cut it short. `finish_run` on a run stopped meanwhile answers with it, still stopped. A run in a response has each case's line from the queue with its start, seconds and model cost in the run, the run's model cost, and its seconds once it finishes. `finish_run` builds a dispatch's summary from its cases as they stand, counting each case once: Awaiting review with exceptions or clean, flagged by the screening, failed by the agent, and the unfinished ones, Received or In progress, for the next dispatch.

No response the agent reads holds anything from the labels.

With `MODEL_NAME` set when seeding, version 1 is that model at `default`, with no reasoning effort sent; `backend/free_models.py` is the only code that reads `MODEL_NAME`, so set it in `.env` before `npm run seed`. ## Review of the harness

On September 30, 2026, the head of engineering's review (`docs/HUMAN_REVIEW_HARNESS.md`) found that the harness was unfit for a PR:
- The agent had more than 20 hard-coded backend URLs and two ways of calling the backend.
- `queue.py` reached into `main.py`.
- The tools were spread across files, and state was passed through closures instead of LangChain's `context_schema`.
- The instructions were step-by-step scripts.

The agent was rebuilt as `api.py`, `tools.py` and `harness.py`, and the instructions were rewritten as goals. Fresh reviewers then went through the backend, the dashboard and the tests, and their fixes are in the same change.

These problems passed every check. Each phase was built, checked against its criteria and committed, and nobody read the code the way a reviewer reads a PR. `docs/CODE_STANDARDS.md` now sets the structural rules the review found broken. `docs/PROCESS.md` now requires a review by a fresh subagent before every commit, and a design in every plan.
